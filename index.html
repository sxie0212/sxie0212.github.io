Biography

Welcome. I am a Scholarly Associate Professor at the School of Electrical Engineering and Computer Science at Washington State University Everett. My primary dedication is to undergraduate education within the Bachelor of Science in Electrical Engineering (BSEE) program.

I earned my Ph.D. in Electrical Engineering from Washington State University in Pullman, WA, where my dissertation focused on atmospheric pressure cold plasma processing. Prior to that, I received my B.E. in Nuclear Engineering from North China Electric Power University in Beijing. As an educator, my goal is to equip the next generation of engineers with both the rigorous theoretical foundation and the practical, real-world skills necessary to thrive in modern industry.

Teaching Philosophy: Engineering Beyond the Textbook

My teaching methodology revolves around moving past textbook abstraction. Engineering is inherently applied, and my classrooms reflect that reality through historical context, hands-on laboratories, and direct industry integration.

Bridging Theory and Industry Practice

I integrate methods connecting engineering education directly to real-world problem-solving. By regularly inviting engineering professionals into my classes for tech talks, mentorship panels, and site tours, students gain invaluable perspective on how the equations they learn solve actual problems in the workforce.

Contextualizing Complexity

Complex theories can often feel disconnected from reality. I utilize historical anecdotes to demystify advanced concepts—such as explaining the development of Laplace transforms in Circuits II or the origins of Fourier analysis in Signals and Systems. Showing the human element and the original problems these engineers were trying to solve makes the mathematics intuitive and approachable.

Current Course & Laboratory Offerings

I am responsible for teaching core 300-level courses and managing laboratory operations (electric circuits, power, and computer labs) at WSU Everett:

  • EE 321: Electric Circuits II (Laplace domain analysis, analogue filter designs)
  • EE 331: Electromagnetic Fields and Waves (Static fields)
  • EE 341: Systems and Signals (Fourier transform, continuous and discrete analysis)
  • EE 361: Electrical Power Systems (Three-phase AC, transformers, rotating machines)
  • EE 352 & EE 362: Electrical Engineering Laboratory I & Power System Laboratory I

Research Focus: Atmospheric Pressure Cold Plasma

While my primary focus is instruction, I maintain an active involvement in research, particularly in atmospheric pressure cold plasma material processing, and I am deeply committed to student research mentorship.

Material Processing

My academic research focuses on atmospheric pressure cold plasma processing of biochar and the inactivation of bacteria. I have investigated various plasma admixtures and generated reactive oxygen and nitrogen species (RONS) in scalable corona discharge reactors for varied processing applications.

Student Mentorship

I actively co-advise Electrophysics major Ph.D. students in projects such as Schlieren-based investigations of Helium flow through pervious metal electrodes. I also guide undergraduate research, including recent projects comparing various Printed Circuit Board (PCB) fabrication methods.
